Guide Resource 01

What is an Intergenerational Program?

At the heart of successful intergenerational programs is the development of positive, ongoing relationships between younger and older people. Rather than a one-off visit or performance, authentic intergenerational practice creates shared spaces where both age groups participate as equals.

Core elements of effective programs

  • Reciprocity: Activities are chosen so both older adults and children give and receive attention, knowledge, and joy.
  • Consistency: Regular sessions allow familiarity, trust, and genuine friendships to blossom over time.
  • Intentional Environment: Spaces are set up for eye-level interaction, comfort, and sensory accessibility.
Toolkit Resource 02

Benefits & Practical Impact

By bridging the generational gap, our programs strengthen community cohesion and contribute to stronger, better-connected communities, increasing engagement and breaking down age-related stereotypes.

Evidence-based outcomes

  • For Older Adults: Elevated mood, cognitive stimulation, increased physical movement, and a renewed sense of community role.
  • For Children (0–5): Rich language exposure, patience, empathy towards people with diverse abilities, and comfort around ageing.
  • For Caregivers & Staff: Supportive peer networks, shared joy at work, and stronger ties between local care homes and families.
Story Resource 03

Creating #AWorld4AllAges

We are a proud supporter of the World Health Organisation’s #AWorld4AllAges—a global campaign to combat ageism and foster solidarity between generations.

Ageism affects both younger and older people when society separates us by age. Through everyday moments—reading a picture book together, passing a shaker during a song, or sharing morning tea—intergenerational programs replace assumptions with real human connection.
